# Corrections and scope of the orbifold results

The numbers in the first three tables refer to David Jaz Myers’s *Orbifolds as microlinear types*. The last table concerns his *Good fibrations through the modal prism*, which uses the Dedekind reals. In the orbifold paper $R$ denotes the smooth line, $\Im$ the infinitesimal modality, and DP compactness means Dubuc–Penon compactness. “Agda” identifies a result checked under the hypotheses of the linked module. “Paper” identifies a mathematical argument outside that formalization. [Orbifolds, arXiv version 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15887v1) · [Good fibrations, arXiv version 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.08034v4)

## Foundation, infinitesimals and microlinearity

| Axiom 2 | Retain contractible shape of $R$, hence the implication from abstract discreteness to $R$-nullity. The converse conflicts with K, O.1–O.2, and J and W for a particular finite Weil algebra. Its infinitesimal variety has an endomorphism invariant distinguishing the identity from the constant map. | Paper |
| Axiom 4 with Definition 4.3.1 | Nullifying only at the full Penon disk also conflicts with crisp pullback preservation, K, O.1–O.2, Constancy and J and W for the same Weil algebra. This contradiction is independent of Axiom 2. Use nullification at **all crisp finite Weil varieties**, as proposed in Remark 4.3.2, and assume it preserves crisp pullbacks. | Paper. | Axiom 3, J and W, and Axiom 5 | J is used for a fixed crisp finite Weil algebra, allowing ordinary coefficients and maps. W applies to crisp infinitesimal varieties. In the formal smooth sheaf interpretation the unrestricted varying-algebra form of J, tininess of the full Penon disk, and DP compactness of the closed interval fail. The revised main theorem dispenses with interval DP compactness. The full Dubuc site also cannot supply the retained shape adjunction. Its constant-object functor fails to preserve a countable product on the Cantor locus. | Paper and sheaf models. [Revised axioms](README.md#axioms-of-the-revised-foundation) |
| Lemma 4.1.3, Remark 4.1.6 and Definition 4.2.1 | A finite presentation gives an augmented Weil algebra with the same pointed spectrum, in the first universe above the ring. This comparison uses function extensionality, truncations and propositional extensionality in every universe. It proves the comparison needed for tests and nullification. | Agda. The ordinary inverse function theorem supplies the chart in the usual formal smooth model. | Agda in the forward direction. Paper and sheaf interpretation for the converse. This proof removes the earlier use of tiny types and preservation of pushouts or sequential colimits. Applying it to every crisply discrete **higher** group would require an additional modal-loop argument. | Agda. ## Finiteness, properness and the main theorem

| Statement or question | Correction or resolution | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definition 6.1.3, second sentence, and Remark 6.1.4, first sentence | Use the first sentence of the definition. A properly finite set has decidable equality and is merely a quotient of a subtype of a finite set. Shulman’s Lemma 8.15 requires a **crisp carrier** for the implication from discreteness to decidable equality. For Remark 6.1.4, a finitely enumerable set with decidable equality is finite. Hom-sets at ordinary endpoints need their own equality decision. | Paper for the erratum. Agda for the definition and chart argument. [Shulman, Lemma 8.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.07584v3) · [Proper finiteness](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/ProperFiniteness.lagda) |
| Remark 6.1.4, does properly finite imply subfinite? | **No.** Over the complex plane the family of nonzero square roots of $z$ has decidable equality and a subfinite enumeration. A local embedding into a finite set near the origin would label the two roots compatibly around a loop, contrary to monodromy. | Paper, counterexample in the smooth sheaf model |
| Warnings 6.1.6 and 6.3.5 | The binary quotient $Q_z$ is the image $\{0,z\}$ in $R$. Its two labels coincide exactly when $z=0$, so a uniform equality decision would decide vanishing on $R$. The set is DP compact, finitely enumerable and $R$-null under E and Constancy. Calling this last property abstract discreteness uses the discarded converse of Axiom 2. | Paper |
| Proposition 6.1.9(5)–(6) | The image of a subfinitely enumerable set in a set with decidable equality is properly finite. Finite unions of properly finite subsets are properly finite when the ambient set has decidable equality. Subfinite enumeration alone supplies only the enumeration part of proper finiteness. | Paper |
| Theorem 6.2.13 and Remark 6.2.16 | With natural numbers starting at zero, use $1/(n+1)$ in the countable-cover argument. For a crisp subset with DP-compact point type in the specified countably based space, a crisp open cover merely admits a finite indexed subcover. Repeated indices are allowed. | Agda. [Countable covers](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/CoveringProperty.lagda) · [Finite subcovers](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/FiniteSubcovers.lagda) |
| Warning 6.2.20, must a crisp Penon-closed bounded set be DP compact? | **No.** The set consisting of $0$ and the points $1/(n+1)$ admits a positive function taking value $1$ at zero and $1/(n+1)$ at the corresponding nonzero point. It has no uniform positive lower bound. Closure of DP-compact types under all Penon-closed subtypes is equivalent to full internal excluded middle. The corresponding question for double-negation-closed subtypes remains open. | Paper |
| Lemma 6.2.21 and its use at the end of Theorem 6.3.7 | A DP-compact subset of an ordinary manifold **with decidable equality on the subset** is properly finite. In the printed argument discreteness is used to infer equality decisions even when the subset varies. The family $\{0,t\}$ exhibits the gap. The revised proof obtains decidable equality on each hom-set from injective source charts. | Paper for the corrected lemma. Agda for the new hom-set proof. [Finiteness from charts](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/CoverFiniteness.lagda) |
| Lemma 6.3.4, including the first paragraph of the proof | An all-Weil-modal subtype of a **crisp ordinary manifold** is $R$-null. Every smooth-real curve in it is constant. Only square-zero displacements are needed. E, Exp and Constancy give local constancy in charts, a finite chain handles crisp curves, and sharp introduction handles ordinary curves. This dispenses with Axiom 5. Neither $\flat$-discreteness nor decidable equality is established by the argument. The revised main theorem bypasses the lemma. | Paper |
| Questions after Warning 6.3.5 | **Yes, under the paper’s Axioms 3 and 5.** Every locally constant map from $R$ to a set is constant. A union of two $R$-null subsets is $R$-null, as is the quotient of two labels identified under any given proposition. If two inhabited subsets cover $R$, they overlap. A second proof of the curve conclusion of 6.3.4 under these extra hypotheses permits ordinary ambient manifolds. | Conditional paper proofs. The revised core omits Axiom 5. |
| Definition 6.3.6 and Theorem 6.3.7 | For ordinary manifold pregroupoids, assume ordinary local source charts and a cover-proper anchor. This provides microlinearity of the completion and properly finite identity types. A DP-proper anchor with formally étale source forces open singletons and decidable equality on objects and arrows, and implies the revised hypotheses. The converse fails for the unit groupoid of $R$. The half-turn groupoid on $R^2$ also fails the DP hypothesis. | Agda for the main theorem and strict comparison, using set-indexed covers. Paper for the half-turn obstruction. [Exact hypotheses](README.md#the-revised-main-theorem) · [Forward comparison](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/ProperEtaleGroupoids.lagda) · [Counterexample](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/SmoothLineGroupoid.lagda) |
| Other sufficient replacements for §6.3 properness | A finite family of pairwise disjoint source-injective charts over each member of a base cover gives subfinite hom-sets. Crisp Penon-localic properness together with a source-injective atlas gives properly finite hom-sets. Both furnish the finiteness conclusion. The released theorem uses the neighbourhood condition below. | Paper |
| Properness at §6.3, finite control over a fibre | The hom-set proof uses finitely many source charts covering a single fibre. Cover-properness requires those opens to cover the **whole inverse image of some open neighbourhood**. In ordinary Hausdorff manifolds the neighbourhood condition is equivalent to properness. In the smooth sheaf interpretation the internal fibre test at a **generalized** base point provides a neighbourhood with a fixed finite list. Deriving that implication over the abstract core alone is open. | Paper for scope. [Formal cover predicate](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/CoverProperness.lagda) |
| Properness at §6.3, stability under base change | Cover-proper maps compose. Without crisp completeness there is also a chartwise product theorem for $p:E\to B$ multiplied by the identity of $T$ when $\dim T+\dim E\le2$ and $\dim B\le2$. Crisp base change along ordinary open embeddings or ordinary local homeomorphisms with admissible chart covers preserves cover-properness. With **completeness of the crisp reals**, arbitrary crisp continuous maps between crisp ordinary manifolds with supplied crisp countable atlases and bases preserve it by base change, using the induced pullback topology. Completeness here means every crisp nested sequence of nonempty rational closed intervals has a crisp point in their intersection. | Paper, conditional for arbitrary manifold maps |
| The same base-change question over the core alone | **No** for unrestricted base change in a sheaf model. The point $0:1\to R$ is cover-proper. Pulling it back along a smooth map with Cantor zero locus gives an inclusion for which a crisp interval cover has no finite control near an external Cantor point outside the model’s field of crisp reals. The pullback is a nonmanifold. Without completeness, stability when the pullback remains an ordinary manifold is open. | Paper, sheaf-semantic counterexample |
| Theorem 6.4.1, Proposition 6.4.2, Definition 6.4.3 and Corollary 6.4.4 | A crisp finite group acting on a microlinear set gives an orbifold quotient. Pullbacks of orbifolds are orbifolds. The inertia type of an orbifold is an orbifold. Each uses the adopted microlinearity tests, and the quotient theorem uses the all-Weil modality. The pullback result here concerns **orbifolds themselves**, independently of base change for cover-proper maps. | Agda. [Finite-group quotients](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/FiniteGroupQuotients.lagda) · [Pullbacks and inertia](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/MyersMicrolinearity.lagda) |

## What the points in §2.4 classify

The following identifications and resulting orbifold statements have paper proofs over the five-item foundation. The general finite-group quotient theorem used in several arguments is checked in Agda. [Myers, §§2.2–2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15887v1) · [The formal quotient theorem](../typetopology/source/gist/Orbifolds/FiniteGroupQuotients.lagda)

| Example | Conditions on the point description |
| --- | --- |
| 2.4.2, elliptic points | Require an inhabited $n$-cycle in the unit circle of the Hermitian line. The displayed closure and inner-product conditions of 2.2.10 also admit the empty set. The resulting triples present $R^2/C_n$ as a homotopy quotient. |
| 2.4.3, Satake coordinate patches | For a crisp finite subgroup $\Gamma\le O(n)$ use an inhabited $\Gamma$-subtorsor of the **orthonormal** frames. Transport is then the prescribed orthogonal action. |
| 2.4.4, unlabelled configurations | The pair type presents the permutation homotopy quotient of $X^n$. Its orbifold proof requires $X$ to be a microlinear **set**. Repeated points are allowed. |
| 2.4.5–8, elliptic curves | A metrically discrete lattice has **mere free abelian rank two**. A basis is not part of a point. Rational rank two alone admits varying subgroups with no mere integral basis. The line-and-lattice type presents the upper half-plane modulo $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. |
| 2.4.9, pillowcases | Twist the additive lattice by the unit-frame torsor of the real line. The expression for the twisted lattice cannot be read as an ordinary tensor product of $R$-modules, since the lattice need not be an $R$-module. |
| 2.4.10, Kummer points | Use quadratic **field** extensions as exemplars. The split quadratic algebra has the wrong point data. The canonical involution is $z\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(z)-z$, which acts as conjugation. |
| 2.4.11, teardrops and weighted projective types | Use strictly positive integral weights. A zero weight admits a point with full circle isotropy. Finite ineffective isotropy is allowed, so coprimality is unnecessary. |
| 2.4.12, crystallographic torus quotients | For a crisp finite subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$ use an inhabited subtorsor of **integral** frames. The associated action on the torus is then the indicated integer-matrix action. |

## Good fibrations through the modal prism

These statements concern abstract shape and Dedekind real cohesion in Myers’s other paper. The countermodels concerning Theorem 8.6 and higher connectedness have paper proofs. Their interpretation as nonderivability for the full native language requires a soundness theorem. [Myers, *Good fibrations through the modal prism*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.08034v4)

| Statement or question | Correction or resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Lemma 4.9(3), Theorem 4.10 and Corollary 9.14 | The use of Shulman’s Lemma 8.15 requires decidable equality on the set of shape components $\int_0 X$, as holds for crisp $X$ under the stated hypotheses. Corollary 9.14 survives because the orbits of a permutation of a finite set have decidable equality. |
| Remark 6.5 | For Dedekind reals, the uniform implication from $r\ne0$ to *r* # 0 is equivalent to the analytic Markov principle. It fails in an ordinary sheaf model of real cohesion, crisp excluded middle and sharp choice. Verified in unpublished Lean. |
| Theorem 8.6, does the comparison extend to ordinary connected types at $n=0$? | **No** in the scalar-end sheaf model. The extension would imply $R$-nullity of the binary quotient by every proposition, hence overt connectedness of the real line for arbitrary subsets. Preservation of ordinary **zero-connectedness** itself still holds by Lemma 8.5. |
| Question in §8, does shape preserve ordinary $n$-connectedness for $n\ge1$? | **No** in a sheaf-of-spaces model with two-dimensional ends. An $n$-connected iterated delooping has shape that fails to be $n$-connected. Both models in these last two rows satisfy crisp excluded middle and $R^\flat$ and refute Axiom T. The corresponding questions with T remain open. |
